# Why people stop, look and engage: the psychology behind our posts

Research note, October 2026. Peer-reviewed studies first, industry evidence flagged as such.
The point is practical: every finding ends in a rule we apply to Eaglescroft and Joshua posts.

## The short version

1. **Recognition beats imagination.** People process what they already know faster, and fluent
   processing feels good and trustworthy. Real logos, real app screens, real photos and familiar
   icons win over abstract graphics.
2. **Faces stop the scroll.** Photos with faces get more likes and comments, and *where the face
   looks* steers where the viewer looks.
3. **On mobile, people read.** In the feed, text gets more attention than pictures. The
   headline does most of the work; the visual earns the stop.
4. **Arousal drives sharing.** Awe, amusement, surprise, constructive worry ("am I missing
   this?") and warmth get shared; flat, sad or purely informational content less so, except
   on LinkedIn, where useful information wins.
5. **Curiosity needs a hint.** Headlines that are *somewhat* concrete outperform both vague
   clickbait and full summaries.
6. **Decisions happen in a blink.** Visual appeal is judged in about 50 ms, and that first
   impression colours how credible everything else feels.
7. **Culture changes how people look.** Some audiences scan for one focal object, others
   read the whole scene. We design for both.

## 1. Familiarity and fluency: why real logos and screenshots work

- Things that are easier to perceive (higher contrast, clearer, seen longer) are judged more
  pleasant. Fluency itself feels positive [Reber et al. 1998](https://consensus.app/papers/details/7cc25f90463a5636b34d68dfe95d0a83/?utm_source=claude_desktop).
- Easy-to-read statements are judged **more likely to be true** than hard-to-read ones
  [Reber & Schwarz 1999](https://consensus.app/papers/details/b00b565b4a0e5d33b0c84d04237ac5af/?utm_source=claude_desktop).
  Legibility is persuasion.
- Familiarity reduces uncertainty, and reduced uncertainty raises liking [Lee 2001](https://consensus.app/papers/details/dbf548f2f8ee5030851799d487014058/?utm_source=claude_desktop).
- Repeated exposure raises liking along an inverted U: it rises, then wears out
  [Montoya et al. 2017, meta-analysis of 268 curves](https://consensus.app/papers/details/3a7ecd8eb0585b3da0cc38bdaddb9deb/?utm_source=claude_desktop).
- Things we see often are assumed to be widely known, which feeds trust [Kwan et al. 2015](https://consensus.app/papers/details/02bf19a88f795aa09315f3e4b562c188/?utm_source=claude_desktop).

**What this means for us**
- When a post is about ChatGPT, WhatsApp or Canva, **show the real thing**: the real logo
  and a real screenshot of the app on a phone. The viewer recognises it in milliseconds, and
  the familiarity carries over to the post and to us.
- Keep our own series formats consistent (same eyebrows, colours and layouts), so Eaglescroft
  becomes familiar too. Vary the content inside them to avoid wear-out.
- Sharp, high-contrast visuals: no muddy low-contrast text, no blurry screenshots.

## 2. Faces and gaze

- Across 1.1 million Instagram photos, pictures with human faces were **38% more likely to get
  likes and 32% more likely to get comments**. Number, age and gender of faces made no
  difference [Georgia Tech / Yahoo Labs, Bakhshi et al. 2014](https://www.news.gatech.edu/news/2014/03/20/face-it-instagram-pictures-faces-are-more-popular).
- When a model looks **at the product**, viewers spend longer on the product, the brand and the
  rest of the ad than when the model looks at the viewer [Hutton & Nolte 2011](https://consensus.app/papers/details/82f5879e188c5ee19642c88cb630eff7/?utm_source=claude_desktop);
  face presence plus gaze toward the product improves attention, memory and purchase intent
  [Adil et al. 2018](https://consensus.app/papers/details/490a85f7efd35028a4428f2796e15cab/?utm_source=claude_desktop).
- **Direct gaze** raises spokesperson credibility for *informative* messages; averted gaze pulls
  people into *emotional* stories [To et al. 2021](https://consensus.app/papers/details/d7e7df98936452e68a8075b0dbc84fb7/?utm_source=claude_desktop).
  A smile plus direct gaze lifts arousal, more strongly for Chinese than American viewers
  [Wang et al. 2017](https://consensus.app/papers/details/c6933b3689335093bd4b5a7552ee863d/?utm_source=claude_desktop).
- Real human endorsers beat virtual ones on attitude and purchase intent [Du 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/1c4fa4fdf08657e9aa177869167c50ec/?utm_source=claude_desktop).

**What this means for us**
- Joshua's face belongs on personal posts as often as possible: his face is a scroll-stopper
  *and* a trust signal.
- **Teaching or showing a tool:** photograph him looking at the phone or screen in the post, so
  the viewer's eyes follow to the content. **Opinion or credibility:** direct eye contact.
- Prefer real people (team, clients with consent, Pexels photos of real people) to AI-generated
  humans or cartoon avatars.

## 3. On mobile, people read

- On a mobile newsfeed, users paid **less attention to visuals and more to text** (eye
  tracking, N = 201, 2024) [Social Media + Society](https://doaj.org/article/5c220c37d5b14fbdb09887b7fde584d3).
- Textual ad elements get more attention than pictorial ones, and the gap is bigger on mobile;
  mobile ads get less attention than PC ads overall [Xie et al. 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/b4c2f0c10586506f85eec1077feeea2f/?utm_source=claude_desktop).
- Phone users spend less time on each post than desktop users, but intent to click and share
  is the same [UGA Grady eye-tracking study](https://grady.uga.edu/?p=3920).
- The popular "8-second attention span" figure has no credible source. Attention depends on
  the task [ProfileTree review](https://profiletree.com/social-media-attention-span-statistics/).

**What this means for us**
- The headline is the product. 5–9 words, readable at thumbnail size, highest contrast on
  the canvas.
- The visual's job is to *stop* the thumb (a face, a recognisable app, a bold object), then
  hand over to the words.
- One idea per slide. If a line can't be read in two seconds on a phone, cut it.

## 4. Emotion and sharing

- Across three months of *New York Times* articles: content evoking **high-arousal emotions**
  (awe, anger, anxiety) was shared more; low-arousal (sadness) less. Practical usefulness,
  interest and surprise also raised sharing [Berger & Milkman 2012](https://consensus.app/papers/details/a4f1e63c9c8d59f099ef0485a508c61a/?utm_source=claude_desktop).
- Video ads: amusement, excitement, inspiration and warmth drive sharing; **prominent early
  branding hurts sharing**; emotional ads are shared more on Facebook and X, while
  **informational ads do better on LinkedIn** [Tellis et al. 2019](https://consensus.app/papers/details/f0d81d061f725127b6a47aac3f75be3a/?utm_source=claude_desktop).
- On WeChat (387,000 articles, 6M users): **anxiety, love and surprise** spread furthest; anger
  and sadness dampened spread [Yu et al. 2020](https://consensus.app/papers/details/8419cfa2f17e53bea2ce2f6b486df2d0/?utm_source=claude_desktop).
  Discrete emotions such as amusement and "cute" also predicted Facebook sharing
  [Paletz et al. 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/5553571a4e4f5b78973b8642e6218b65/?utm_source=claude_desktop).
- Positive posts with photos are shared more (UK universities, 1.27M tweets)
  [Yang et al. 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/a351d55f859c57efae4fa34e19698389/?utm_source=claude_desktop).

**What this means for us**
- Pick the emotion before the design. Our best fits: **awe** ("look what this tool can do"),
  **surprise** ("you've been using it wrong"), **constructive worry** ("is your data safe?"),
  **amusement** (a light meme-style slide), **warmth** (a trader's real win).
- Avoid outrage. It spreads, but it attracts mutes and blocks, which cut reach, and it's off-brand.
- LinkedIn: lead with usefulness. Facebook / Instagram / X: lead with feeling.
- Keep branding quiet. A small brand bar at the bottom, never a giant logo at the top.

## 5. Curiosity: the right size of gap

- Across **8,977 headline A/B tests**, click-through follows an inverted U: headlines that are
  too vague *or* too complete underperform; the winners give a concrete hint and hold back
  the payoff [Aubin Le Quéré et al. 2025](https://consensus.app/papers/details/ad7c9a66ab355c38ae48e9502a5b8d20/?utm_source=claude_desktop).
- Curiosity drives clicks, but clickbait lowers likes per click, so it doesn't build loyalty
  [Qiu et al. 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/b46a148a6a55585eb3ad7a8e941579d5/?utm_source=claude_desktop);
  people *read* on curiosity but *share* on perceived quality (Pengnate et al. 2021, *Journal of International Technology and Information Management*).
- Curiosity ads work best with a gap, a hint and an assured payoff [Menon & Soman 2002](https://consensus.app/papers/details/0c5ca803c979550da88913034a6bf898/?utm_source=claude_desktop).

**What this means for us**
- Headline formula: **specific detail + open loop + promise of a payoff**.
  ✓ "Beyond ChatGPT: 6 AI tools worth knowing" · ✓ "13,000 raw records. 12,000+ scored leads."
  ✗ "You won't believe this AI trick" (too vague) · ✗ "Use Perplexity for cited research" (no gap).
- Carousels: the cover opens the loop, slide 2 pays something off immediately, and the last
  slide closes it with a save or follow prompt.

## 6. First impressions in a blink

- People judge a page's visual appeal in **50 ms**, and those judgements match their slower
  ones [Lindgaard et al. 2006, via Nature](https://www.nature.com/news/2006/060109/full/news060109-13.html).
  The first impression spills over into credibility.
- Eye tracking (via secondary sources) suggests users fixate on photos with real information
  and skip generic filler stock; vendor A/B tests report lifts when real people replace stock
  [summary, not peer-reviewed](https://www.getelastic.com/people-in-photos).

**What this means for us**
- Clean layouts, one focal point, generous space and a consistent palette: the "this looks
  professional" verdict happens before anyone reads.
- No generic stock clichés (handshakes in suits, robots shaking hands). Real people in real
  settings, real screens.

## 7. How different cultures look

- Eye tracking across five cultural groups: Eastern viewers scan more **holistically**
  (relationships, context), Western viewers more **analytically** (one focal object); cultural
  background mattered as much as geography [Scientific Reports 2023](https://doaj.org/article/1dd643de77164b07b63a5c6e482da281).
- Instagram, US vs Indonesia: US users rewarded **one salient focal object** (fewer people, strong
  figure-ground contrast); Indonesian users rewarded **balanced composition**, with brightness and
  contrast working together [ITAA 2025 proceedings](https://www.iastatedigitalpress.com/itaa/article/id/22016/).
- Both US and Chinese consumers liked ads with several types of information; Americans liked
  single-attribute ads more [same review](https://www.iastatedigitalpress.com/itaa/article/22016/galley/19398/download/).

**What this means for us** (Eaglescroft is global; Joshua's base is Nigeria, a collectivist,
community-first culture)
- Design for both: **one clear focal object** (analytic eyes) placed in a **balanced, readable
  scene** with context cues (holistic eyes).
- Show people *together*: teams, a trader with customers, a lecturer with students. Community
  framing ("send this to a trader you know") fits collectivist audiences.
- Local context where it matters: Nigerian settings, Pidgin captions on Facebook, WhatsApp
  as the hero app. Global proof elsewhere: London, Lagos and Sydney side by side.
- Nigerian reach (DataReportal / NapoleonCat 2026): about 48M social media users, Facebook
  ~46M, Instagram ~9.5M, WhatsApp used by over 95% of internet users (2024 survey)
  [DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-nigeria) ·
  [NapoleonCat](https://stats.napoleoncat.com/instagram-users-in-nigeria/2026/06/) ·
  [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1176101/leading-social-media-platforms-nigeria/).

## 8. Using real logos and screenshots responsibly

- Logos are trademarks. Using a logo to **refer to** a product in education or commentary is
  normal; using it in a way that **implies partnership or endorsement** is the risk
  ([OpenAI brand guidelines](https://openai.com/brand/); an
  [OpenAI community reply](https://community.openai.com/t/logo-questions-about-license/440259) calls a
  side-by-side lockup with your own logo non-standard).
- Rules we follow:
  - Use official logo files, unaltered, in their own colours or plain white/black. Never recolour into our blue.
  - Never place a tool's logo beside the Eaglescroft logo as if we were partners.
  - Screenshots are our own captures of the real app, with nothing faked and private data blurred.
  - Some brands (OpenAI/ChatGPT, Canva, CapCut, LinkedIn) asked the open-source icon project
    to remove their logos, so get those from their official brand pages, or show their app
    in a real screenshot instead.
